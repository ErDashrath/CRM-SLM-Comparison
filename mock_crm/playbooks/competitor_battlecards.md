# Competitor Battlecards

## XYZ

**Category:** Direct competitor, mid-market and enterprise operations platform
**Primary competitive motion:** Pricing-aggressive. XYZ typically enters late in a deal cycle with a quote 10-20% below list on comparable scope, often after the prospect has already completed a technical evaluation with us.

### Strengths (their side of the story)
- Lower sticker price, usually pitched as a straight line-item comparison against our list price.
- Fast initial implementation timeline claims (though these frequently slip in practice — see below).
- Willing to heavily discount to win a logo, especially against an incumbent vendor.

### Weaknesses (our angle)
- **Implementation support is materially weaker.** XYZ's post-sale implementation team is thin relative to their sales motion; customers who switch commonly report longer-than-promised go-live timelines and limited hands-on support during rollout.
- **Support SLAs are business-hours only** even on their higher tiers — no equivalent to our Premium Support 24x7, 4-hour response option.
- **Shallow integration ecosystem.** Their pre-built connectors cover fewer ERP/SCM systems than our Integration & API Module; customers often end up paying for custom integration work that wasn't in the original quote.
- **Discount-then-anchor pattern.** Their aggressive initial pricing is not sustainable at renewal — customers report renewal quotes reverting toward list price once the account is locked in, without a corresponding increase in dedicated support.

### How to position against XYZ
1. Do not match their price line-for-line — reframe on total cost of ownership (implementation risk, support responsiveness, integration completeness), consistent with the Negotiation Playbook.
2. If a prospect cites a specific XYZ quote, ask what implementation timeline and support tier is included — this is usually where the comparison breaks down.
3. Reference existing customers who evaluated or switched from XYZ, where appropriate, to substantiate the implementation-quality gap.
4. Escalate any XYZ-driven discount request through the standard Discount Policy approval thresholds — competitive pressure alone does not justify exceeding the approved band.
