# Discount Policy

**Owner:** Sales Operations
**Applies to:** All Nimbus Systems commercial-stage opportunities
**Last reviewed:** 2026-06-01

## Approved negotiation band

For standard enterprise renewals and expansions, sales reps are authorized to offer a discount of **5% to 7% off list price** without further sign-off. This is the band we expect the large majority of negotiated deals to close within, and it is the number account executives should anchor to when procurement opens a pricing conversation.

## Hard ceiling

**10% is the hard ceiling.** No discount above 10% off list price may be offered or verbally indicated to a customer under any circumstances without Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) sign-off, regardless of deal size, competitive pressure, or how close the deal is to the forecast close date. This is a guardrail, not a guideline — reps who commit to a discount above 10% without CRO approval are expected to walk it back, even if that creates an awkward conversation with the customer.

## Approval thresholds

| Discount requested | Approval required |
|---|---|
| 0% – 7% | Account Executive discretion (within approved band) |
| Above 7%, up to 10% | VP of Sales approval required before the number is communicated to the customer |
| Above 10% | CRO approval required; treat as an exception, not a negotiation tactic |

## Why the ceiling exists

Discounting beyond 10% has historically correlated with margin erosion that is very difficult to claw back at renewal, and with customers anchoring future renewals to the discounted rate rather than list price. A competitor undercutting on price is not, by itself, sufficient justification to exceed the approved band — reps should first exhaust non-price levers (see the Negotiation Playbook) before escalating a discount request.

## What to do when a customer asks for more than the band

1. Do not confirm or imply a number above 7% in the moment.
2. Re-engage the economic buyer directly — pricing pushback from a procurement contact alone, without economic-buyer involvement, is often a signal that the value case hasn't been made at the right level, not that the price itself is the blocker.
3. If a genuine competitive threat is documented (e.g., a specific competing quote), escalate to the VP of Sales with the competitive detail attached before offering anything above 7%.
4. Any ask at or above 10% should be flagged as a risk in the CRM opportunity record, not just handled silently in a phone call.
